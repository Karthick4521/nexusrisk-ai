INVESTIGATION PRINCIPLES

1. Evidence Requirement
Every finding presented to an investigator must be traceable to one or
more specific transaction IDs from the actual uploaded data. The system
must never fabricate or imply evidence that is not present in the
supplied transaction history.

2. Customer-Specific Baseline
Before any transaction can be judged "unusual", the customer's own
historical behavior must be established first: typical amounts, typical
hours, typical channels, and known beneficiaries. Global thresholds
alone are not sufficient because normal behavior varies significantly
between customers.

3. Separation of Fact and Interpretation
Every explanation provided to an investigator should distinguish:
  - Observed Fact: a plain statement of what is in the data
    (e.g. "TXN1042 has an amount of Rs 75,000").
  - Rule Finding: which deterministic rule fired and why
    (e.g. "R01 triggered because this exceeds the customer's typical
    range").
  - Interpretation: what this might mean in context.
  - Recommendation: what the investigator should check next.
  - Uncertainty: what information is missing or cannot be determined
    from the supplied data.

4. AI Limitations
The AI assistant (Gemini) is used only for language reasoning,
explanation and summarization. It does not calculate statistics, does
not decide which rules fire, and does not determine review priority
scores. All numerical decisions are made deterministically in Python
before the AI ever sees the case.

5. Human Review Requirement
The system never states that fraud has occurred. It flags, explains,
connects, and recommends. The final judgement on any case always
belongs to a qualified human investigator.
